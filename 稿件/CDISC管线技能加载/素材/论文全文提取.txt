Abstract
Background: Clinical trial data pipelines must follow Clinical Data Interchange Standards Consortium (CDISC) standards,
including the study data tabulation model (SDTM) and analysis data model (ADaM). General-purpose large language models
(LLMs) can produce plausible artifacts that omit required variables, misuse terminology, or invent derivation logic. Curated
context may reduce these errors, but the value of organizing that context by workflow topology has not been separated from
corpus access alone.
Objective: This study aimed to examine when workflow topology–guided skill loading adds value beyond a domain prompt,
nonrouted curated context, or lexical retrieval, and whether bounded scheduling retains quality with less injected context.
Methods: A 45-node, 7-layer directed acyclic workflow graph was linked to 59 modular rule files (SKILL.md). A sequential
exploratory evaluation examined 4 evidence regimes spanning domain-context, component isolation, lexically reachable, and
topology-dependent conditions (10 tasks, 90 runs; 10 tasks, 180 and 120 runs; 24 tasks, 288 runs; and 18 tasks, 324 runs; the
task, not the agent run, was the inferential unit). End points included LLM rubric scores, deterministic atomic criteria,
required-evidence recall, and manifest checks. A blinded practicing statistical programmer independently rescored 60 outputs.
Task-level paired inference used bootstrap 95% CIs, exact sign-flip tests, and Holm adjustment.
Results: Routed context scored 4.63 versus 4.20 for prompt-only (difference 0.43 on a 1-5 scale; 95% CI 0.25-0.63; Holm-adjusted
P=.01) but did not exceed flat or random loading. The lower-context scheduler used 28.82% less context while retaining quality;
all 559 injected fragments had recorded sources, and all 120 manifests were reproduced exactly. On lexically reachable tasks,
graph+Best Match 25 (BM25) and BM25-only were equivalent within a 0.05 margin (difference +0.009; 95% CI –0.028 to 0.046;
P=.67), where BM25 already recalled 95.8% of required evidence. On topology-dependent stress tasks, graph-only selection
outperformed BM25-only (difference 0.348; 95% CI 0.178-0.530; nominal P=.002; d =0.89) and raised required-evidence recall
z
from 0.500 to 1.000. Expert-judge agreement was substantial for terminology (weighted κ=0.61) and moderate overall (intraclass
correlation coefficient=0.60).
Conclusions: Workflow topology serves as a conditional retrieval prior. It adds little when lexical retrieval already reaches the
required evidence but recovers dependency-linked evidence when task wording does not identify it lexically. These findings,
from a synthetic benchmark using one generator backbone and one expert rater, support topology-guided selection as a promising
approach for controlled context assembly and motivate independent factorial validation.
